      How to drag click

      Drag clicking means sliding your finger along the mouse button so the friction makes it vibrate. Each tiny bounce of the switch registers as a click, so one drag can give 5, 10 or 20+ clicks.

      1. The mouse

        Not every mouse can drag click, so check yours first.

        • Mechanical switches. Drag clicking relies on the metal contacts in the switch bouncing. Optical switches use a light beam instead and don't bounce, so they top out at 1–2 clicks per drag.
        • Low debounce time. Debounce is a filter that ignores clicks that come too close together, and it eats the bounces you're trying to make. Look for a mouse whose software lets you set it, ideally to 0–4 ms.
        • A light, crisp click. Buttons that need little force to press, with a short travel, vibrate more easily.
        • A smooth or matte button surface. Your finger (or grip tape) needs something to rub against. Heavily textured or rubberised buttons can damp the vibration.
      2. Set it up

        Open your mouse's software and set debounce (sometimes called "click delay") as low as it goes. Make sure you changed the active profile, then unplug the mouse, plug it back in and check the setting stuck.

      3. Finger position

        Tilt your finger to about 45° and use the very tip, or the side of the tip, not the flat pad. Brace the mouse with your thumb and pinky so it doesn't slide around under the drag.

      4. Pressure

        This is the hardest part. Press only just hard enough for the switch to sit right at its click point while it vibrates. Too light and your finger buzzes on the button without clicking; too hard and the button just stays held down.

        • Buzz but few clicks: press slightly harder.
        • Smooth slide with no buzz: press lighter.
      5. The drag

        Drag from the top of the button down towards the front edge in one smooth, medium-speed motion. Too slow gives no friction; too fast ends before it builds up. Use the whole length of the button, and if it isn't working, try a different spot, nearer the front edge or the side, or a diagonal direction.

      6. Feel for the buzz

        A working drag feels and sounds like a rattle or grind, like rubbing a finger on a balloon or a clean plate. A steady, even buzz means a good drag. Once you can feel it, the click count usually jumps quickly.

      7. Be consistent

        Start every drag from the same spot, at the same angle, with the same pressure. When you get a great drag, notice what was different and repeat it on purpose. Slow down while learning; speed comes back once it's consistent. The Coach and the per-drag bars in Drag practice mode show how consistent you are.

      8. Fast resets

        Once your drags are consistent, work on the gap between them: lift and start the next drag in one smooth loop. Aim for 3+ drags a second. Your CPS is roughly clicks per drag × drags per second.

      9. Practise smart

        Short sessions with rests beat long grinds, and a lot of the improvement happens overnight. Use "Start session" in the Practice panel and stop when your drags fade. Track progress with 10-second timed tests, since 1-second tests swing a lot.

        • 6–10 CPS: normal clicking
        • 15–25 CPS: decent drag clicking
        • 25–40+ CPS: very good drag clicking
      10. Grip tape

        Grip tape on the main button gives your finger something to grab, so it vibrates the button instead of slipping over it. Thin tape works better than thick, soft tape, which absorbs the vibration. Keep the tape and your fingertip clean; a very slightly damp fingertip grips better than a bone-dry one.

      Some game servers limit or flag very high CPS, so check the rules where you play.

      Hand tracking

      A camera watches your clicking hand while you drag. After each drag the app tells you what your finger did: its angle, where the drag started, how far and fast it moved, and whether it lifted. The video stays on your PC. It's analysed live and never saved or sent anywhere.

      1. Use your phone as the camera (recommended)

        A webcam points at your face, but this needs a side view of your hand on the mouse. A phone is easy to place and usually has a better camera.

        • Android and Windows 11: open Settings → Bluetooth & devices → Mobile devices, allow this PC to access your mobile devices, and link your phone with the Link to Windows app. On recent Windows 11 versions the phone's camera then appears in the camera list below.
        • Any phone, iPhone or Android: install a free phone-webcam app such as Iriun Webcam, DroidCam or Camo on both your phone and your PC. Connect them over the same Wi-Fi, or a USB cable for less lag, and the phone appears as a camera.
        • If the app has a 60fps option, turn it on. More frames per second means more detail in each drag.
      2. Place the camera
        • Turn the phone on its side (landscape), level with the top of your mouse, about 20–30cm away.
        • Film from the side, so the camera sees your clicking finger side-on. For a right-handed mouse, put it on the left.
        • Keep your whole hand in shot, including your wrist.
        • Use good lighting, and prop the phone up so it can't move.
      3. Start the camera

        A preview appears next to the click pad, with the measured part of your finger in green.

      4. Calibrate

        This tells the app where your button is in the picture. Press Mark back, then within 3 seconds put your clicking fingertip on the back of the button (the end nearest your palm) and hold it still. Then do the same with Mark front and the front edge of the button. Calibrate again if you move the camera.

        Seen from the side, as your camera sees it. The orange line is the button.

      5. Record your drags (optional)

        Saves short clips of your 3 best and 3 worst drags from each session, with what made them good or bad, so you can watch them back in slow motion. Clips are saved only on this PC, and the last 10 sessions are kept.

      6. Drag

        Go back to practice and use Drag practice mode or a practice session. The Coach adds what your hand did after each drag. After 8 tracked drags it starts comparing your best and worst drags, to show what actually works for you, and a green circle on the preview marks where your best drags start.

      The camera can't see pressure or the vibration itself, so some problems won't show up on video. Measurements in cm are rough, based on an average hand size.
